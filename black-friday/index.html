How to tell if a Black Friday deal is real

  1. Ignore the "was" price. Stores often raise it in October. Compare against the usual price from the last 3 months instead (price history sites, or a screenshot you took earlier).
  2. Count everything you pay. Shipping, tax and a required membership can wipe out a 15% discount.
  3. Check the model number. TVs and laptops sold only on Black Friday are often cheaper "derivative" models with lower specs.
  4. 20% off the real usual price is a great deal. 8-20% is OK. Under 8% is a normal price with a sale sticker.
  5. Buy from your list, not the ad. Set a budget first and only buy what was already on it.

What is usually cheapest on Black Friday

TVs, headphones and earbuds, smart speakers, video game console bundles, small kitchen appliances (air fryers, stand mixers), toys and LEGO, and streaming or software subscriptions. Phones, furniture and winter clothes are often cheaper at other times of year.

How it is worked out

You pay: sale price minus the coupon, plus shipping, plus tax on the item (most states tax the price after the coupon), minus cash back on the price before tax. Real saving: the usual price with the same tax and shipping, minus what you pay. "Store says" is the discount from the "was" price. A great deal saves at least 20% against the usual price, an OK deal 8-20%; under that it is a normal price with a sale sticker.
